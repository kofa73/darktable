My first experiment would fit the missing channel directly from the surviving channels in linear camera RGB. If that works, extend it by transporting the fitted coefficients through large clipped regions. This combination has the clearest route to improving skies while retaining detail in lights: it changes the channel relationship, rather than finding another way to interpolate boundary hue. **This is a research recommendation, not a demonstrated improvement over inpaint opposed.**

I would rank the following four experiments. These are proposed adaptations, all **I**. The first two are successive tests of one promising combination, not two unrelated mechanisms.

1. **Direct local regression, with a censored statistical estimate. Highest priority.**

   On a half-size camera-RGB grid, fit `missing = intercept + a * survivor1 + b * survivor2` from genuinely unclipped samples in overlapping windows. Use ridge regularization and robust residual weights. For a single clipped channel, compare ordinary prediction with the one-dimensional truncated-Gaussian estimate from Zhang–Brainard. For two clipped channels, use a joint conditional model with a small constrained MAP solve; do not treat a previously reconstructed channel as an independent measurement.

   This differs materially from per-region opposed offsets: both slopes are fitted, predictions vary with the measured channels, and fitting uses a neighborhood rather than one offset for a connected clipped segment. It also differs from the parked log-brightness proposal: there is no fitted sky-color-versus-brightness curve. Censoring is an observation model for the missing channel, not a lower bound inserted into a hue interpolation.

   Start with daylight skies having two surviving channels, then one-survivor skies, and compact lights with surviving-channel texture. Test window sizes on a small development subset. Artificially clip trusted bright samples to check prediction error, while recognizing that this cannot validate an unseen material inside the real hole. Compare with ordinary opposed and the existing per-region-offset variant.

   **Success:** Pass the common criteria below, with a gain beyond per-region offsets. **Failure:** Improvement exists only in artificial clipping, or extrapolated slopes produce new sky casts or noise. **Effort:** 3–5 developer days for a CPU prototype and ablations. Bounded windows and small fits should fit comfortably in memory; measure time rather than assume it.

2. **Transport fitted coefficients using surviving-channel structure. Best extension if experiment 1 succeeds.**

   Build slope/intercept anchor fields from reliable fits, then fill missing coefficients across the clipped region. Compare isotropic coefficient filling with anisotropic filling guided exclusively by measured surviving-channel structure. Evaluate the transported model directly on those channels. Ansel provides a checked implementation reference, but its full pipeline need not be ported.

   The difference from Laplace hue, convolution-pyramid hue, and Masood-style hue propagation is the unknown being transported: a measured channel relationship. Within-region guide detail still determines the reconstructed value. This can preserve light texture without copying an occluder's RGB value across the border. It can still carry the wrong relationship across a boundary invisible in every surviving channel.

   Test the largest connected skies, sky fragments between branches, roof/pole boundaries, and the light-source pairs where gradient domain wins. Keep the fully clipped-core treatment fixed. Compare against experiment 1; match solver convergence before attributing a gain to directional transport.

   **Success:** Better sky scores and boundaries than experiment 1 while retaining the required light gains. **Failure:** It merely relocates the pink/cyan contamination, adds seams, or needs a different tuned transport rule for each scene. **Effort:** An additional 1–2 weeks. Reduced coefficient grids and regional buffers make the resource limits plausible. Patent avoidance remains unestablished.

3. **Joint sparse declipping of RGB values. A separate, lower-confidence route.**

   Implement a reduced-grid version of Mansour's idea: minimize a joint RGB wavelet-coefficient sparsity penalty while retaining trusted observations and imposing saturation inequalities at observed clipped samples. Use an analysis formulation with a primal-dual solver, overlapping regions, and a fixed iteration budget. Joint shrinkage can favor shared channel structure without first estimating boundary hue. This joint-color formulation is a proposed variant, not a claimed reproduction of the paper.

   Start on broad neutral clouds, including clouds cut by branches; include textured colored lights as adversarial controls. Hold fully clipped-core behavior fixed in the first comparison. Compare against experiment 1 and opposed. Inspect cloud texture and edge halos, not just average color.

   **Success:** Pass the common criteria within a bounded iteration count. **Failure:** Flat minimum-energy skies, erased colored detail, ringing, or unacceptable scaling when a sky covers half the image. **Effort:** 1–2 weeks for a useful prototype. Expect tens to hundreds of image passes; runtime is the main reason for its lower rank. No training data needed. No specific associated patent identified, with clearance still unresolved.

4. **A small, single-pass learned residual model. Longer-term option.**

   Train a compact network on camera-RGB grids plus per-channel clipping masks and camera/WB metadata. Predict residuals only for clipped channels; copy trusted samples exactly. Use wide, low-resolution context with a small local refinement stage. Start with a sub-million-parameter design and benchmark ONNX CPU inference before expanding it. This is not a proposal to ship full RawHDR or the patent-pending recurrent method.

   Training needs independent unclipped raws artificially exposed and clipped *before* demosaicing, with varied cameras, CFA patterns, noise, illumination, skies, and emitters. Real brackets are also needed to catch sensor rolloff and synthetic-clipping mismatch. The 103 evaluation pairs are too small and already too heavily consulted to establish broad generalization. Keep them out of final-model training; scene-grouped cross-validation on them would be only an exploratory feasibility test.

   Test every usable pair, particularly sunset skies, colored lights, white objects, and genuinely all-channel-clipped cores whose darker reference is still valid. Bundle approved weights locally if adopted; no runtime download is needed.

   **Success:** Pass the same quality/resource criteria across held-out cameras, with no invented clouds or repeated light texture. **Failure:** Gains disappear on real clipping or unseen cameras, or require generative detail that looks plausible but disagrees with the darker frame. **Effort:** 4–8 weeks plus training-data work. CPU feasibility and model licensing must be established, not assumed.

- Use all 103 usable pairs for the final exploratory comparison. Define `L` as the compact-light pairs among gradient domain's existing wins; do not assume all 26 wins are lights. Keep the existing tie rule. Report results against opposed, per-region opposed offsets, and gradient domain separately.
